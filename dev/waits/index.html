How the port waits¶

Every call that waits for hardware is bounded, measured on the port's own clock, and asleep under an RTOS. No wait of the port reads HAL_GetTick(), and none spins the CPU at the caller's priority.

The rule¶

Wait Started by Ended by Bounded by
I2C transfer HAL_I2C_Master_Seq_Transmit_IT / _Seq_Receive_IT / _Master_Transmit_IT (probe) The HAL's completion or error callback I2cConfig::timeout
SPI chunk HAL_SPI_TransmitReceive_IT / HAL_SPI_Transmit_IT The HAL's completion or error callback SpiDeviceConfig::timeout per chunk
UART write The transmit-empty interrupt (the port's own handler) The last byte handed over, or transmit-complete with RS-485 UartConfig::write_timeout
UART read — The receive interrupt (a byte or an error) The Read() timeout
WaitTxDone() The transmit-complete interrupt Transmit-complete Its timeout
ADC conversion HAL_ADC_Start_IT HAL_ADC_ConvCpltCallback or HAL_ADC_ErrorCallback AdcConfig::conversion_timeout
CAN receive — The RX FIFO 0 interrupt (into the port's ring) The Receive() timeout
CAN send (no free slot) — The transmit-complete interrupt The Send() timeout
CAN bus-off recovery Recover() The controller back on the bus (checked after each pause) The Recover() timeout
Locks — kb::rtos::Mutex The object's lock timeout

Every timeout, configured or per call, is at most kMaxTimeout (2 s); a longer one, and HAL_MAX_DELAY, is refused with InvalidArgument. 2 s leaves room for a secure element that stretches SCL for several hundred milliseconds, and is short enough that a failing bus cannot hold callers for long. A caller that must wait longer loops.

Deadline and Completion¶

Deadline (completion.hpp) measures a budget on the port's Clock (TIM2 or TIM5) once one has been started, and on the DWT cycle counter (extended to 64 bits before any division, never written) before that. A second, independent bound ends a wait even if that time source stops (an application that re-initialises or gates the clock's timer, for example):

  • under FreeRTOS (KB_RTOS=FREERTOS, CMake defines KB_PORT_STM32_RTOS_BLOCKS), the time slept on the RTOS tick: each slice that ended by its timeout lasted at least the slice, so the deadline expires after budget + kWaitSliceMs of slept slices (a 50 ms wait at most 60 ms of RTOS time);
  • under kb-core's none backend, where a take cannot sleep, the poll count: (budget in ms + 1) x kDeadlinePollsPerMs polls.

Completion is armed by the task, completed from the interrupt (or the HAL callback) with the vendor code, and waited for in slices of at most kWaitSliceMs (10 ms) on a kb::rtos::Semaphore:

  • FreeRTOS (KB_RTOS=FREERTOS): the task blocks in the semaphore take until the interrupt gives it or the slice ends. It uses no CPU while it waits, and lower-priority tasks run.
  • none backend (KB_RTOS=NONE, bare metal): kb-core's none backend has no scheduler; its take cannot block and returns at once (its SleepFor does not sleep on silicon either: its tick is a fake counter). The wait becomes a poll of the completion flag, bounded by the Deadline on the port clock or the DWT and by the poll cap. That is the documented fallback: correct and bounded, but it occupies the CPU for the wait, as any bare-metal wait does.

On a timeout the operation is stopped before the call returns: an I2C transfer has its handle restored (and the peripheral reset if BUSY stays set), an SPI transfer is aborted (HAL_SPI_Abort, a counted wait in the HAL) and the peripheral initialised again before the next frame, an ADC conversion is stopped (HAL_ADC_Stop_IT), a UART write has its transmit interrupts disabled.

What still depends on the HAL tick¶

The HAL's own initialisation and stop paths wait on HAL_GetTick() for single hardware acknowledgements that take microseconds on healthy silicon: HAL_UART_Init (TEACK/REACK), HAL_ADC_Start_IT's first enable (ADRDY) and HAL_ADC_Stop_IT, HAL_FDCAN_Init/HAL_CAN_Init and their start and stop (INIT acknowledge). The port does not replace the HAL there; instead:

  • Uart, CanController and AdcChannel refuse EnsureReady() with NotReady (port code kHalTickStopped) when the HAL time base does not advance within two HAL tick periods (HAL_GetTickFreq()) and at least 25 ms, measured on the DWT, so a HAL tick advanced at 100 Hz (from an RTOS tick hook) is accepted; Uart::SetBaud() and the FDCAN restart in CanController::Recover() check it again before their HAL calls;
  • call EnsureReady(), Release() and SetBaud() from task context, never with interrupts masked, never inside a critical section, and never from an interrupt;
  • under an RTOS, give the HAL its time base from a source that runs whenever tasks run (see Getting started).

The I2C and SPI initialisations and every transfer do not use the HAL tick.

Two waits spin the CPU at the caller's priority by design, both bounded and outside the transfers: the HAL's I2C v1 (F4) busy-flag wait at the start of a transfer (a counted loop, about 25 ms of iterations, only when BUSY is set), and the HAL time-base check of EnsureReady() (ends at the first tick, at most 25 ms or two tick periods).

Later: kb-core's asynchronous Start/Poll contract¶

kb-core is adding an asynchronous Start/Poll contract for hot paths (its design, section 16: "a non-blocking form" for bus calls). The port is built so that it maps onto it without a new HAL path:

  • every interface call is already split into a start (sdk::I2cStartTransmit, sdk::SpiStart, sdk::AdcStart, the transmit-interrupt enable) and an end signalled by an interrupt into a Completion;
  • Start() would arm the Completion and call the same start; Poll() would return Done() and, when done, the result from Code() (and Value() for the ADC), without waiting; a timeout would be the caller's deadline on the same port clock, and the same stop-and-restore runs when the caller gives up;
  • the blocking calls stay, written as Start() followed by Completion::Wait().

The change is then local to the platform-free files (i2c.cpp, spi.cpp, adc.cpp, pwm.cpp, uart.cpp, can.cpp); the boundary files and the HAL callbacks stay as they are.
